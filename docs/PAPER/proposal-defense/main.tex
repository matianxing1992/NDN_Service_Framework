\begin{abstract}
A requester may know the required service without knowing which authorized Providers are currently available and willing to execute it. The initial NDNSF prototype supports discovery and selection of one Provider per invocation over Named Data Networking (NDN), but not multi-role collaboration. This proposal evaluates that foundation and investigates its extension to dependent service roles.

In the evaluated design, attribute-based encryption (ABE) protects discovery descriptors without enumerating Provider identities. ABE-backed challenge verification, signature validation under trust rules, and request-state checks govern service authorization. Service permissions remain independent of identity-signing credentials. Authorized but unselected candidates can read descriptors; request-scoped keys protect selected input and result exchanges. The proposed collaboration extension uses eligible positive acknowledgment (ACK) Data to assign roles and derive local dependency checks. Providers are intended to accept intermediate Data only from assigned predecessors over declared edges, whether retrieved from producers or caches. Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) multi-view sensing and distributed inference provide application settings for testing the extension.

Existing evaluations support limited invocation and execution-efficiency findings, not the effectiveness of the proposed collaboration mechanism. Planned evaluation compares complete authorization alternatives, tests role and dependency enforcement during execution and recovery, and measures communication, processing, and management costs. The research aims to establish when the invocation design and its collaboration extension are useful, and when their costs outweigh their benefits.
\begin{keywords}
Named Data Networking, service authorization, provider discovery, collaboration, distributed inference
\end{keywords}
\end{abstract}
